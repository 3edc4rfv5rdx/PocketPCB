# PocketPCB

A small, simple editor for schematics and double-sided printed circuit boards with manual routing. The aim is a board you can order from a factory (Gerber and drill files) or make at home by toner transfer (1:1 layer printing). It is not a KiCad competitor: few features, kept simple.

Written in C++ with Qt 6. Linux first; the code is kept portable. Text is drawn in a built-in stroke font, Newstroke from KiCad, the same on any computer. The interface is in English, Ukrainian and Russian, following the system or chosen in Settings > Language (taken at the next start).

## Status

Early development. What works now:

- Projects: the project folders lie in a common PocketPCB-Projects folder beside the program (beside the AppImage file), where saving and opening start until another place is used; Settings > Projects folder chooses another. Each project lives in a folder of its own: `amp/amp.ppcb` (project), `amp.ppsch` (schematic), `amp.ppbrd` (board), all JSON. Saving keeps backup copies in `backup/`, the open project is autosaved to `autosave/` and offered back after a crash, and you are asked before unsaved changes are lost; the bottom left corner of the window shows in colour whether the project is saved, and a click on it saves. The File menu lists the recent projects. How each page was last looked at (zoom and position) is kept in a hidden `.ppview` beside the project file.
- Project properties (File > Project properties): the fields of the title block (an empty date prints the day of the last save), and the designator style, international (R, D, Q, U) or GOST (R, VD, VT, DD); a new style renames the parts as one step, numbers kept, a designator that would repeat getting the first free number; the net classes with their track width, clearance and via, added, renamed (everything giving the class follows), deleted (its nets go to the default or the power class, asked first), and which is the default class and the power class.
- No menu bar: the menus (File, Edit, View, Settings, Help with About) open from the menu button at the left end of the strip of tools, or by F10; their keys work all the same.
- Strip of tools over the canvas, its pictures drawn by the program: the menu, Recent projects, Save, Print schematic, Export schematic to PDF, Undo, Redo, then Zoom in and out, Fit the page (Home), Fit the drawing (Shift+Home, what is drawn without the empty paper), Actual size (Ctrl+0, by the screen size set in Settings > Screen size, else as the screen reports it); in the middle Lock, which locks or unlocks what is chosen, then a gap and the modes of the page shown (the one on shown pressed): on a sheet Select, Wire, Bus, Label, Power, No-connect, on the board Select, one button for the outline (Rectangular board, Cut-out or Outline, the one chosen last from its arrow) and Update board from schematic (F8); on the right the switches of the Library, Properties, Nets and ERC panels and the BOM; each tip names its key. Rotate, mirror and delete are in the menus and on their keys.
- Library: a built-in library and your own (`~/.local/share/PocketPCB/library`), plus the library of the open project in `amp/library/`. Parts, symbols and footprints are JSON files you can copy by hand. A project keeps copies of the parts it uses, so it opens the same even when the library changes. File > Update parts from library shows which of them differ from the library (fields, symbol, moved pins, footprints) and takes those ticked as one step; wires stay where they are, components with no rated power get the part's.
- Built-in parts: resistors, NTC and PTC thermistors, a photoresistor, a varistor, capacitors, trimmer and variable capacitors, an inductor, a choke with a core, a ferrite bead, a transformer (plain and with winding starts marked), a relay (SRD), an optocoupler (PC817), a trimmer, diodes (rectifier, Zener, Schottky, varicap, TVS one- and two-way, a bridge), a photodiode and LEDs, bipolar, Darlington, field-effect, MOS, IGBT and unijunction (KT117) transistors, a thyristor, a triac, a diac, DIP chips, voltage regulators (78xx, 79xx, LM317), a single op amp, a 555 timer, a crystal, a fuse, a resettable PTC fuse, a gas discharge tube, a Hall sensor, a thermocouple, reed switches (make and changeover), an antenna, a button, a buzzer, a microphone, a motor, an incandescent and a neon lamp, 0.56" seven-segment displays (common cathode and common anode), pin headers, screw terminals, connector pins and sockets (->, -<), a wire pad, a test point, a wire link (7.62, 10.16, 15.24), vacuum tubes (ECC83, 6N2P, EL84 / 6P14P on a noval socket, 6L6 / 6P3S on an octal one), and panel parts (jacks, switches, a potentiometer, a speaker, a battery). Symbols follow GOST/IEC style.
- Library panel: search (by name, file name or designator prefix), parts grouped by what they are (resistors, capacitors, … , the rare ones such as RF and vacuum tubes last) after a group of power symbols, a drawing of the symbol and footprint; a double click on a part on the sheet shows its part and footprint there.
- Power symbols are library symbols with a net name: a bar, an arrow, a ground and a chassis are built in, and your own are added as files. Symbols with one name are one net, whatever they look like.
- Sheets: tabs over the canvas, the board last. A sheet is added with "+" or from a tab's right-click menu, renamed with a double click on its tab, moved by dragging its tab; the menu also chooses A4 or A3 paper, landscape or portrait, and deletes a sheet, asking first when something is on it; View > Sheet does the same for the sheet shown.
- Schematic sheet:
  - the colour of the paper is chosen from a table of pale ones in View > Sheet background, the sheet taking the colour under the mouse as a preview; it is kept with the program, not in the project;
  - choose with a click or a rectangle (a part needs only its drawing inside, not its texts); a click on one of several chosen objects keeps it alone; what is chosen stays chosen after a change;
  - Lock (on the strip, in the menu of the sheet as Lock or Unlock, or Locked in the Properties panel) fixes the chosen parts and power symbols: they are not moved, turned, mirrored, deleted or cut until unlocked, by the mouse or a key, their texts still move, and a copy of one is not locked;
  - drag along the grid with the wires following (Shift tears them off), rotate and mirror with the wires following too, delete, copy, cut, paste and duplicate (pasted parts are numbered on); the wires are laid again by the cheapest way round bodies, pins and other wires (Settings > Wire routing > Around obstacles, the default) or by trying a few ways with one or two bends (Simple); Settings > Keep a history of steps writes each step, with the schematic and the board before and after it, into `~/.cache/PocketPCB/history/` (the newest 100), to show what went wrong in one;
  - draw wires (W; Space turns the corner, / or . slants it) with junction dots placed by themselves; the menu of a point where wires cross offers Join wires, which puts a dot there, and that of a dot where they go on straight across it offers Separate wires, which leaves them crossing;
  - place power symbols from the panel, or with P and the Power button, which place the last one again (the chassis at first) and show it in their picture, the arrow beside the button choosing another; wires join them like pins, and a double click edits the net name in place;
  - place net labels with L: a click asks for the text, the next label is offered numbered on (D0, D1, …); a label on no wire and no pin shows white on a red plate, one on a wire or a pin goes with it when that moves; a click on its text chooses the text alone and a drag moves it away from the point, which stays and shows as a small hollow diamond; a double click edits its text;
  - draw buses with B, the way wires are drawn: each is named BUS1, BUS2 … at first, and a double click renames it (D[0..7]); it is dragged by its segments and ends like a wire and chosen whole with a double click, the wires entering it going along; its name is chosen and dragged alone, like a part's designator; a bus drawn to another bus ends there and branches off it, with its name and a dot at the T, going along when that bus is dragged; Shift and a click choose a bus with its branches; a bus shows its name wherever "Add name here" in its menu puts it, each place dragged or deleted alone, the last of a bus and its branches kept; a wire drawn to a bus ends on it, entering it at 45 degrees over its last grid step, and joins the others by its label;
  - put no-connect marks on pins with X: a mark goes only on a pin of a part, is red elsewhere, goes with the pin when the part moves, and is deleted with the part;
  - a click on a wire chooses its segment: a drag moves it across, Delete removes it, and taken near the wire's end it drags that end, unless the end is on a pin; wires drawn on from each other end to end become one wire, also on a pin, up to a fork or a bus, unless one goes back along the other, and a wire drawn back over itself is laid as wires covering each stretch once, so a double click chooses that whole line, Shift and a click every wire joined to it; an end of a dragged segment or whole wire that lies on another wire slides along it; a dot where wires end on the middle of another is taken near it and dragged along that wire, round its corners, the wires ending there following from their far ends; a pin a dragged wire end, dot or segment, or a wire going with it, passed through keeps a wire to it;
  - a click on a part's designator or value chooses that text alone, and a drag moves it; an empty value shows as a grey VALUE mark, except on parts that need none, such as switches and connectors; a double click on the value edits it in place;
  - the mouse resting a second on a wire, a pin, a label or a power symbol lights up its whole net on the sheet, with a dot on each of its pins, and shows the net's name beside the cursor;
  - a short right click opens a menu, framed to stand out from the paper: on an object it chooses it first (what is chosen already stays chosen) and offers turning, mirroring, deleting, copying, cutting, duplicating and editing its value, net name, label text or bus name in place; on empty paper it offers pasting, wires, buses, labels, power symbols and selecting all;
  - Undo/Redo.
- Properties panel: the designator, value, footprint (one the part allows), off-board flag, locked flag, comment (not drawn on the sheet) and, for resistors, rated power of the chosen components; with GOST designators the power is marked inside the resistor's body (/// // / — | || and Roman numerals from 5 W), with international ones it is written after the value unless most resistors have it; it is also chosen from the menu of the sheet; with several chosen, what is set goes to them all as one step.
- Nets panel: every net with its name, class and pins, and a filter by name or pin. A net's class is chosen from a list; a net with no label gets a lasting name (NN1, NN2 …) kept by its pins when a class is chosen for it or the board is updated from the schematic, else a passing one (TempN1, TempN2 …), shown grey with a tip saying so, and two nets with classes joined by a wire take the stronger class. A row chosen lights its net, opening a sheet it is on; Esc on the sheet drops it.
- BOM panel, beside the others, shown by View > BOM or its button on the strip of tools: the parts of the schematic, those alike (part, value, footprint, rated power and comment) in one row, with their designators and how many, under a heading for each group of parts (resistors, capacitors and so on; parts without a group last, under Other); the rated power most resistors have is said once below the list, another one follows the value; the value and the comment are typed in the table for the whole row, a double click on the designators shows those parts, and the buttons write the list as `out/<project>-bom.txt`, `.csv` or `.pdf`.
- Schematic on paper, black on white with its frame and title block: File > Print schematic (Ctrl+P) asks the printer, and the sheet shown, all sheets or a range, each turned and sized to fit the printer's paper; File > Export schematic to PDF writes `out/<project>-schematic.pdf`, every sheet on a page of its own paper 1:1, asking first whether the sheet shown or all.
- ERC panel: the schematic is checked after every change; each problem is listed under its sheet and ringed in red there: a missing value, a repeated designator, a pin joined to nothing, a no-connect mark on no pin or on a joined one, a label or a wire end joining nothing, a wire lying along another, wires closing a loop, a two-pin part with both pins on one net, a net with two names, a name of the nets table with a class and no pins left, or given to another net. The rings show while the panel is open. A problem chosen shows its sheet, centred on it, and an object chosen on the sheet chooses its problem in the list; a missing value opens for typing at once.
- File > Update board from schematic (F8) lists what will change and makes it one step: every component on the board with a footprint gets a placement tied to it (renumbering keeps the tie), packed in a block of columns to the right of the board outline; placements whose component is gone, off the board or without a footprint are removed, while one whose footprint is missing from the project library is kept as it is and said so in the list; designators, values, footprints and the nets of the pads follow the schematic, while positions stay.
- The board is drawn on black: top copper red, bottom copper blue, pads through the board gold, vias violet, the top silkscreen (footprint drawings, designators, texts) white, the bottom one grey, the outline lilac; light green lines join the pads of each net still to be joined (the ratsnest), each pad to the nearest one of its net, following the parts as they are dragged; its grid is 1.27 mm in a new project. A cyan corner (an arm along X, one along Y) marks the origin the coordinates count from; it is not printed.
- Board outline: Rectangular board asks for the width and the height; Outline draws a new one corner by corner, every corner on any grid point, the sides at any angle, a click on the first corner closing it; the coordinates of the corner being drawn or dragged stand beside it (View > Coordinates while drawing the board); Cut-out takes a rectangle drawn across the edge off the board, the corners it makes on a slanted edge going to the grid (refused, with the reason in the status bar, when it misses the board, lies inside it or would split it). In Select a corner or an edge under the mouse is lit; a corner is dragged along the grid, an edge is dragged along the grid, both its corners alike, the edges beside it stretching after it (a level edge only up or down, an upright one only sideways), and a double click on a corner types its radius in place. A double click on an edge chooses the outline whole, and Ctrl+A the outline and every part: a drag on it then moves it all, with the parts chosen, Esc or a click off it lets it go, and Lock fixes it so that it is not changed or moved until unlocked. Each change is one step.
- Board parts: a click on a part chooses it, Ctrl and a click adds it or takes it away, a frame drawn on the empty board chooses the parts wholly inside it; a drag moves the parts chosen along the grid and R turns them by 90 degrees, one about its own point, several about their middle, and while dragged about the point of the one taken; Lock fixes them so that they are not moved or turned until unlocked (their texts still move), and a locked part is drawn yellow on the screen (it stays white on the silkscreen). A click on a designator or a value chooses that text alone, a line joining it to its part while it is chosen (a part chosen shows its texts chosen too, with such lines), and a drag moves it away from the part. Parts are not deleted on the board: they come and go with the schematic.

Planned next: the autorouter, tracks and DRC; Gerber and Excellon export; 1:1 printing for toner transfer.

## Building

Needs CMake 3.22+, a C++17 compiler and Qt 6.4+ (Core, Gui, Widgets, LinguistTools, Test). On Ubuntu 24.04:

```
sudo apt install build-essential cmake ninja-build qt6-base-dev qt6-tools-dev qt6-l10n-tools qt6-translations-l10n
./00-Make.sh
```

`00-Make.sh` configures, builds and runs the tests; a build that passed them is copied to `OUT/pocketpcb`. Open `examples/demo/demo.ppcb` to see a small project.

`14-MakeAppImage.sh` then packs that build into `OUT/pocketpcb-<version>-x86_64.AppImage`, one file with Qt and the built-in library inside, keeping the last three. It needs `linuxdeploy` and `linuxdeploy-plugin-qt` in `tools/` (kept out of git) and says how to get them when they are missing.
