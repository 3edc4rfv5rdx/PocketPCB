# PocketPCB

A small, simple editor for schematics and double-sided printed circuit boards with manual routing. The aim is a board you can order from a factory (Gerber and drill files) or make at home by toner transfer (1:1 layer printing). It is not a KiCad competitor: few features, kept simple.

Written in C++ with Qt 6. Linux first; the code is kept portable. Text is drawn in a built-in stroke font, Newstroke from KiCad, the same on any computer. The interface is in English, Ukrainian and Russian, following the system or chosen in Settings > Language (taken at the next start).

## Status

Early development. What works now:

- Projects: the project folders lie in a common PocketPCB-Projects folder beside the program (beside the AppImage file), where saving and opening start until another place is used; Settings > Projects folder chooses another. Each project lives in a folder of its own: `amp/amp.ppcb` (project), `amp.ppsch` (schematic), `amp.ppbrd` (board), all JSON. Saving keeps backup copies in `backup/`; File > Save version asks what changed, saves the project and writes it whole (project, schematic, board, library, and `version.json` with the number, time, revision and description) as the next zip in `versions/`, `0003-20261125-revB.zip`, and Gerber export does the same with the description Gerber when the files changed since the newest version; the open project is autosaved to `autosave/` and offered back after a crash, and you are asked before unsaved changes are lost; the bottom left corner of the window shows in colour whether the project is saved, and a click on it saves. File > Open last project (Ctrl+Shift+O) opens the last project, and File > Recent projects lists the last seven. A project opened while the board is shown opens on its board, else on its first sheet. How each page was last looked at (zoom and position) is kept in a hidden `.ppview` beside the project file.
- Project properties (File > Project properties): the fields of the title block (an empty date prints the day of the last save; the revision, printed as "Revision: B", has a Next button giving the one after it), and the designator style, international (R, D, Q, U) or GOST (R, VD, VT, DD); a new style renames the parts as one step, numbers kept, a designator that would repeat getting the first free number; the copper sides of the board: both, top only or bottom only (choosing one side deletes the tracks of the other and the vias, asked first, and copper on a side the board has not joins nothing); the net classes with their track width, clearance and via, added, renamed (everything giving the class follows), deleted (its nets go to the default or the power class, asked first), and which is the default class and the power class; the rules on the Rules tab: the rule set (Manufacturing or Toner transfer; choosing one fills in its values, the check levels kept, and the board's tracks stay as they are), the track widths typed as millimetres with slashes between (spaces or semicolons do too) and the default one among them, clearance, edge clearance, annular ring, via hole and diameter, wire link hole and pad, solder mask expansion, vias under the mask, the drills and whether only they may be used, and the level of each check (error, warning or off), a hole not smaller than its pad or a list that is not lengths refused.
- No menu bar: the menus (File, Edit, View, Settings, Help with User guide (F1, `help.html` in the browser) and About) open from the menu button at the left end of the strip of tools, or by F10; their keys work all the same.
- Strip of tools over the canvas, its pictures drawn by the program: the menu, Recent projects (a click opens the last project, its arrow lists the last seven), Project properties, Save (on red while something is not saved), Print and PDF (of the schematic, or on the board Print board), Undo, Redo, then Zoom in and out, Fit the page (Home), Fit the drawing (Shift+Home, what is drawn without the empty paper; on the board the parts, those off it too, and the copper, without the outline), Actual size (Ctrl+0, by the screen size set in Settings > Screen size, else as the screen reports it), Brighter grid (the grid drawn much brighter while the button is held); in the middle, on the board, Update board from schematic (F8) and a gap, then Lock, which locks or unlocks what is chosen, then a gap and the modes of the page shown (the one on shown pressed): on a sheet Select, Wire, Bus, Label, Power, No-connect, on the board Select, one button for the outline (Rectangular board, Cut-out or Outline, the one chosen last from its arrow, Rectangular board at first), Hole, Track (T), Layer (its picture two sheets of copper, the side chosen in front; a click goes to the other side as V does, its arrow chooses one); what the board shows is chosen in View and in the menu of the empty board: Hide silkscreen (the silkscreen of both sides hidden, the pads, holes and copper staying, and parts and their texts not chosen), Hide tracks (the tracks and vias hidden, to see what is still to be joined, not chosen and lighting nothing; the ratsnest still counts them, and Track shows them again), Bold unrouted (every ratsnest line bold with a dot at each end), Unrouted as arcs (the ratsnest drawn as gentle arcs, those meeting at a pad bent away from each other); the menu of the empty board also has Spread tracks (each piece between two corners moved across itself, a cell of the board's grid at a time and two at most, wherever that leaves it and the pieces beside it more room to copper of other nets and to the edge, keeping the clearances and never onto copper of its own net it is not joined to; one step) and Delete all tracks, which deletes the vias too as one step, after asking with an orange question mark; the status bar on the board says how many connections are still unrouted of all; on the right the switches of the Library, Properties, Nets and ERC panels and the BOM; each tip names its key. Rotate, mirror and delete are in the menus and on their keys; on the board Delete takes off the holes chosen and moves the parts chosen off the board.
- Library: a built-in library and your own (`~/.local/share/PocketPCB/library`), plus the library of the open project in `amp/library/`. Parts, symbols and footprints are JSON files you can copy by hand. A project keeps copies of the parts it uses, so it opens the same even when the library changes. File > Update parts from library shows which of them differ from the library (fields, symbol, moved pins, footprints) and takes those ticked as one step; wires stay where they are, components with no rated power get the part's.
- Built-in parts: resistors, NTC and PTC thermistors, a photoresistor, a varistor, capacitors, trimmer and variable capacitors, an inductor, a choke with a core, a ferrite bead, a transformer (plain and with winding starts marked), a relay (SRD), an optocoupler (PC817), a trimmer, diodes (rectifier, Zener, Schottky, varicap, TVS one- and two-way, a bridge), a photodiode and LEDs, bipolar, Darlington, field-effect, MOS, IGBT and unijunction (KT117) transistors, a thyristor, a triac, a diac, DIP chips, voltage regulators (78xx, 79xx, LM317), a single op amp, a 555 timer, a crystal, a fuse, a resettable PTC fuse, a gas discharge tube, a Hall sensor, a thermocouple, reed switches (make and changeover), an antenna, a button, a buzzer, a microphone, a motor, an incandescent and a neon lamp, 0.56" seven-segment displays (common cathode and common anode), pin headers, screw terminals, connector pins and sockets (->, -<), a wire pad, a test point, a wire link (7.62, 10.16, 15.24), vacuum tubes (ECC83, 6N2P, EL84 / 6P14P on a noval socket, 6L6 / 6P3S on an octal one), and panel parts (jacks, switches, a potentiometer, a speaker, a battery). Symbols follow GOST/IEC style.
- Library panel: search (by name, file name or designator prefix), parts grouped by what they are (resistors, capacitors, … , the rare ones such as RF and vacuum tubes last) after a group of power symbols, a drawing of the symbol and footprint; a double click on a part on the sheet shows its part and footprint there.
- Power symbols are library symbols with a net name: a bar, an arrow, a ground and a chassis are built in, and your own are added as files. Symbols with one name are one net, whatever they look like.
- Sheets: tabs over the canvas, the board last. A sheet is added with "+" or from a tab's right-click menu, renamed with a double click on its tab, moved by dragging its tab; the menu also chooses A4 or A3 paper, landscape or portrait, and deletes a sheet, asking first when something is on it; View > Sheet does the same for the sheet shown.
- Schematic sheet:
  - the colour of the paper is chosen from a table of pale ones in View > Sheet background, the sheet taking the colour under the mouse as a preview; it is kept with the program, not in the project;
  - choose with a click or a rectangle (a part needs only its drawing inside, not its texts); a click on one of several chosen objects keeps it alone; what is chosen stays chosen after a change;
  - Lock (on the strip, in the menu of the sheet as Lock or Unlock, or Locked in the Properties panel) fixes the chosen parts and power symbols: they are not moved, turned, mirrored, deleted or cut until unlocked, by the mouse or a key, their texts still move, and a copy of one is not locked;
  - drag along the grid with the wires following (Shift tears them off), rotate and mirror with the wires following too, delete, copy, cut, paste and duplicate (pasted parts are numbered on); the wires are laid again by the cheapest way round bodies, pins and other wires (Settings > Wire routing > Around obstacles, the default) or by trying a few ways with one or two bends (Simple); Settings > Keep a history of steps writes each step, with the schematic and the board before and after it, into the `history/` folder of the project once it is saved (the newest 25), to show what went wrong in one;
  - draw wires (W; Space turns the corner, / or . slants it) with junction dots placed by themselves; the menu of a point where wires cross offers Join wires, which puts a dot there, and that of a dot where they go on straight across it offers Separate wires, which leaves them crossing;
  - place power symbols from the panel, or with P and the Power button, which place the last one again (the chassis at first) and show it in their picture, the arrow beside the button choosing another; wires join them like pins, and a double click edits the net name in place;
  - place net labels with L: a click asks for the text, the next label is offered numbered on (D0, D1, …); a label on no wire and no pin shows white on a red plate, one on a wire or a pin goes with it when that moves; a click on its text chooses the text alone and a drag moves it away from the point, which stays and shows as a small hollow diamond; a double click edits its text;
  - draw buses with B, the way wires are drawn: each is named BUS1, BUS2 … at first, and a double click renames it (D[0..7]); it is dragged by its segments and ends like a wire and chosen whole with a double click, the wires entering it going along; its name is chosen and dragged alone, like a part's designator; a bus drawn to another bus ends there and branches off it, with its name and a dot at the T, going along when that bus is dragged; Shift and a click choose a bus with its branches; a bus shows its name wherever "Add name here" in its menu puts it, each place dragged or deleted alone, the last of a bus and its branches kept; a wire drawn to a bus ends on it, entering it at 45 degrees over its last grid step, and joins the others by its label;
  - put no-connect marks on pins with X: a mark goes only on a pin of a part, is red elsewhere, goes with the pin when the part moves, and is deleted with the part;
  - a click on a wire chooses its segment, up to a pin or the end of another wire lying on it: a drag moves it across, Delete removes it, and taken near the wire's end it drags that end, unless the end is on a pin; wires drawn on from each other end to end become one wire, also on a pin, up to a fork or a bus, unless one goes back along the other, and a wire drawn back over itself is laid as wires covering each stretch once, so a double click chooses that whole line, Shift and a click every wire joined to it; an end of a dragged segment or whole wire that lies on another wire slides along it; a dot where wires end on the middle of another is taken near it and dragged along that wire, round its corners, the wires ending there following from their far ends; a pin a dragged wire end, dot or segment, or a wire going with it, passed through keeps a wire to it;
  - a click on a part's designator or value chooses that text alone, and a drag moves it; an empty value shows as a grey VALUE mark, except on parts that need none, such as switches and connectors; a double click on the value edits it in place;
  - the mouse resting a second on a wire, a pin, a label or a power symbol lights up its whole net on the sheet, with a dot on each of its pins, and shows the net's name beside the cursor;
  - a short right click opens a menu, framed to stand out from the paper: on an object it chooses it first (what is chosen already stays chosen) and offers turning, mirroring, deleting, copying, cutting, duplicating and editing its value, net name, label text or bus name in place; on empty paper it offers pasting, wires, buses, labels, power symbols and selecting all;
  - Undo/Redo.
- Properties panel: the designator, value, footprint (one the part allows), off-board flag, locked flag, comment (not drawn on the sheet) and, for resistors, rated power of the chosen components; with GOST designators the power is marked inside the resistor's body (/// // / — | || and Roman numerals from 5 W), with international ones it is written after the value unless most resistors have it; it is also chosen from the menu of the sheet; with several chosen, what is set goes to them all as one step; Properties in the menu of a part shows the panel with the keyboard in its first field (the designator, or the value with several chosen).
- Nets panel: every net with its name, class and pins, and a filter by name or pin. A net's class is chosen from a list; a net with no label gets a lasting name (NN1, NN2 …) kept by its pins when a class is chosen for it or the board is updated from the schematic, else a passing one (TempN1, TempN2 …), shown grey with a tip saying so, and two nets with classes joined by a wire take the stronger class. A row chosen lights its net, opening a sheet it is on; Esc on the sheet drops it.
- BOM panel, beside the others, shown by View > BOM or its button on the strip of tools: the parts of the schematic, those alike (part, value, footprint, rated power and comment) in one row, with their designators and how many, under a heading for each group of parts (resistors, capacitors and so on; parts without a group last, under Other), then the board's wire links under Wire links, a row for each length of bare wire (the distance between the holes) and of insulated wire (that distance and a fifth more, 5 mm at each end, rounded up to 5 mm); the rated power most resistors have is said once below the list, another one follows the value; the value and the comment are typed in the table for the whole row, a double click on the designators shows those parts, and the buttons write the list as `out/<project>-bom.txt`, `.csv` or `.pdf`.
- Schematic on paper, black on white with its frame and title block: File > Print schematic (Ctrl+P) asks the printer, and the sheet shown, all sheets or a range, each turned and sized to fit the printer's paper; File > Export schematic to PDF writes `out/<project>-schematic.pdf`, every sheet on a page of its own paper 1:1, asking first whether the sheet shown or all.
- Board on paper 1:1, for a board made at home: File > Print board (Ctrl+P while the board is shown) asks for the layer (the copper of each side it has, the silkscreen of each side with something on it), Mirrored (the top on at first, the bottom off), Negative for photoresist and the centre holes (0.5 mm at first, no wider than the drill; for the copper), copies and registration marks (crosses outside the board, to put the two sides over each other), then prints, or writes `out/<project>-copper-top.pdf` (`-copper-bottom`, `-silkscreen-top`, `-silkscreen-bottom`) on A4, or A3 for a larger board. The copper is solid black on white, edges not smoothed, sent to the printer in grayscale, with the outline as a thin line and each hole without a pad as a circle inside its edge with a cross over its middle; each page starts with the project, the layer, a 100 mm ruler to check the scale and, for the copper, the drill table (each diameter as it is drilled and how many holes, on as many lines as the page needs); the copies fill the page in rows, turned when more fit so, and go on to the next pages.
- Files for a factory: File > Export Gerber and drill files writes Gerber RS-274X files with X2 attributes into `out/gerber/` — the copper (`.GTL`, `.GBL`) and solder mask (`.GTS`, `.GBS`, openings larger by the rules' mask expansion, vias covered when the rules say so) of each side with copper, the silkscreen (`.GTO`, `.GBO`) of each side with something on it, as the board shows it, and the outline (`.GKO`) — and Excellon drill files, plated (`-PTH.DRL`) and not plated (`-NPTH.DRL`) holes apart, each diameter raised to the nearest drill of the set when only those may be used; all of them also go into `out/<project>-rev<revision>-<yyyymmdd>-gerber.zip` to send to the factory (the revision of the title block, left out when empty); the revision is asked first, Next giving the one after it (3 to 4, A to B), and a new one is written into the project as a step that Undo takes back; the project is then saved and, when it changed since the newest version, kept as a version (Gerber); a box then says where, or what went wrong. A board without an outline is refused.
- ERC panel: the schematic is checked after every change while the panel is open and a sheet shown (else when it is next opened or a sheet shown), not when a project is opened until a change or Check, which checks at once; each problem is listed under its sheet and ringed in red there: a missing value, a repeated designator, a pin joined to nothing, a no-connect mark on no pin or on a joined one, a label or a wire end joining nothing, a wire lying along another, wires closing a loop, a two-pin part with both pins on one net, a net with two names, a name of the nets table with a class and no pins left, or given to another net. The rings show while the panel is open. A problem chosen shows its sheet, centred on it, and an object chosen on the sheet chooses its problem in the list; a missing value opens for typing at once.
- DRC panel, beside the others, shown by View > DRC or its button on the strip of tools: the board is checked after every change while the panel is open and the board shown (else when next it is), not when a project is opened until a change or Check, against the project's rules, each problem listed under Errors or Warnings and ringed on the board while the panel is open (red for errors, orange for warnings): copper of two nets touching (always an error) or nearer than the larger clearance of their classes, copper nearer than the clearance to a mounting hole without a pad, copper nearer to the outline than the edge clearance or out of it, annular rings of pads, vias, wire links and mounting holes narrower than the rules, tracks narrower than the smallest track width or than their class, drills not in the set when only those may be used (one line per diameter), parts whose pads all lie off the outline (left out of the other checks) and connections still to be routed, each at the level the rules give it; the pads of one part are not checked against each other; a problem chosen shows the board centred on it with what it is about chosen, and Check checks again and shows the board.
- File > Update board from schematic (F8) lists what will change and makes it one step: every component on the board with a footprint gets a placement tied to it (renumbering keeps the tie), packed in a block of columns to the right of the board outline; placements whose component is gone, off the board or without a footprint are removed with their tracks (as Delete takes them), while one whose footprint is missing from the project library is kept as it is and said so in the list; designators, values, footprints and the nets of the pads follow the schematic, while positions stay; with another footprint the ends of the tracks go to the pads of the same numbers, the last piece stretching, and the tracks from a pad it lacks, or stretched onto copper of another net, are deleted; the pads of mounting holes follow their nets, renamed with the pads that were on them, or taken off a net that is gone. While two nets with pins have one name (a label or power symbol named like another net's name in the nets table), the update is refused and names them.
- The board is drawn on black: top copper red, bottom copper blue, pads through the board gold, vias violet, the top silkscreen (footprint drawings, designators, texts) white, the bottom one grey, the outline lilac; light green lines join the pads of each net still to be joined (the ratsnest), each pad to the nearest one of its net, none between pads tracks and vias join already, following the parts as they are dragged; the nets of the parts chosen are lit, their pads and their lines; its grid is 1.27 mm in a new project. A cyan corner (an arm along X, one along Y) marks the origin the coordinates count from; it is not printed.
- Board outline: Rectangular board asks for the width and the height (its arrows step a cell of the grid, any size may be typed); Outline draws a new one corner by corner, every corner on any grid point, the sides at any angle, a click on the first corner closing it; the coordinates of the corner being drawn or dragged stand beside it (View > Coordinates while drawing the board); Cut-out takes a rectangle drawn across the edge off the board, the corners it makes on a slanted edge going to the grid (refused, with the reason in the status bar, when it misses the board, lies inside it or would split it). In Select a corner or an edge under the mouse is lit; a corner is dragged along the grid, an edge is dragged along the grid, both its corners alike, the edges beside it stretching after it (a level edge only up or down, an upright one only sideways), and a double click on a corner types its radius in place. A double click on an edge chooses the outline whole, and Ctrl+A the outline and every part, hole, track and via, showing the silkscreen and the tracks again when they are hidden: a drag on it then moves it all, with the parts chosen, Esc or a click off it lets it go, and Lock fixes it so that it is not changed or moved until unlocked. Each change is one step.
- Board parts: a click on a part chooses it, Ctrl and a click adds it or takes it away, a frame drawn on the empty board chooses the parts wholly inside it; a drag moves the parts chosen along the grid and R turns them by 90 degrees, one about its own point, several about their middle, and while dragged about the point of the one taken; the end of a track on the middle of a pad of its net and side, of a part or a hole, follows the pad, the last piece of the track stretching (one shrunk to nothing goes), unless another pad stays there; a track stretched across copper of another net on its side, or nearer to it than its class's clearance, is deleted from pad to pad or fork, its connection going back to the ratsnest; Lock fixes them so that they are not moved or turned until unlocked (their texts still move), and a locked part is drawn yellow on the screen (it stays white on the silkscreen). A short right click opens a menu: on a part or one of its texts Rotate, Lock or Unlock, Show or Hide value, Line to part (a bold silkscreen line from the part's point to each of its texts shown), Show on schematic, Delete; on the outline Lock or Unlock and, on a corner, Round board corner. A click on a designator or a value chooses that text alone, a line joining it to its part while it is chosen (a part chosen shows its texts chosen too, with such lines), and a drag moves it away from the part. Parts are not deleted on the board, as they come and go with the schematic: Delete moves the parts chosen off the board, packed in columns to its right as new ones are (locked ones stay), and deletes their tracks, each from the pad as far as a double click on it would choose.
- Tracks: Track (T) lays them on the side chosen, shown bright, the copper of the other side darker under it. A click on a pad (of a part or of a hole), a via or a track starts one, on the side of that copper, with the net of it and the width of the net's class; every click after it lays a straight piece and one at 45 degrees up to the grid point, or to the middle of the copper under the cursor, as one step (Space puts the slanted piece first or last, Shift lays one piece at any angle), and a click on copper of the side ends it (not on copper of another net, said in the status bar). V puts a via where the cursor is and goes on on the other side, and outside a track chooses the other side; a board with copper on one side lays on that side only. The pads of the net are lit while it is laid. Esc, a short right click or a double click ends the track; Esc again ends laying. Undo while laying takes back the last piece or via and goes on from the point before, on its side; undone back past the start, or with a Redo or another change, laying ends. Undo and Redo of a via, laying or not, bring back the side it was laid from or went to. In Select a click on a track or a via chooses it (off the pads of parts, which choose the part), Shift and a click every track and via joined to it by copper, through pads too, the unrouted connections of its net drawn bold while all of it is chosen, a double click the whole track it belongs to, end to end through corners and vias, up to a pad of a part or a hole and up to a fork (three ends at a point, or a piece with a track ending on its middle), with the vias only its tracks reach, a frame those wholly inside; a drag on a piece of track moves it across itself (a level one up or down, an upright one sideways, one at 45 degrees by whole cells), the piece going on from each end, and a track ending on its middle, keeping its way, made longer or shorter to meet it (a track ending on its middle that cannot meet it again holds the piece where it is) (where it cannot, and at an end on a pad, a via or a fork, a piece straight across joins the old end to the new), pieces then on one line made one and pieces twice over dropped; taken near a corner where two pieces alone meet, the corner is dragged, both pieces laid again to it, straight and at 45 degrees or with Shift straight at any angle; a move that would bring a track nearer to another net than its clearance is refused, said in the status bar; Delete removes them; chosen with parts or holes, they go along whole when those are dragged, unless a part or a hole chosen is locked (a locked outline does not hold them); the menu of a track sets its width (those of the rules, or Other… typed in place) and its side; Spread tracks in it spreads the tracks chosen alone, as the one of the empty board does all. The mouse resting a second on copper lights its whole net (pads, vias, tracks, ratsnest lines) and shows its name beside the cursor; while tracks or vias are chosen, it only shows the name, so they stand out.
- Autorouter: the Autoroute button on the board strip (also Edit > Route all) lays every connection still unrouted, its arrow offering Route all and Route chosen (the nets of the parts, holes, tracks and vias chosen); each connection, the shortest first, is laid by the grid search on a grid of half the board's step and no coarser than 0.635 mm, on both sides of a board of two in eight directions, from any copper of one group (its pads, vias and the tracks laid) to that of the other, so a connection goes on from a track instead of running beside it, keeping the clearance of the classes from copper of other nets and from holes, and the edge clearance from the outline; a via costs as much as a long way round, so it is laid only where needed; on a board with copper on one side a wire link (level or upright, 5.08 to 30.48 mm by 2.54, its pads with room round them, its pads on the board, the edge clearance off it, and outside the parts, its wire, lying on the side of the parts and passing under a raised part, though a way round of up to 20 steps is taken first, kept from every other pad, hole and link by the clearance); where neither a track nor a bare link finds a way, an insulated link goes from wherever its pad fits to within 8 mm of the copper it goes to, dearer still — two links of one way at least the room of their pads and the clearance apart, a track between them leaps what a track cannot pass, dearer than any way round; and on the top level pieces, on the bottom upright ones come cheaper; a step within 1 mm beyond the clearance of copper of another net costs more, so tracks keep away where there is room; the tracks laid are then spread (as Spread tracks does, on the routing grid); what it lays is marked as the autorouter's ("auto" in the file) until dragged or given another width or side by hand; a connection that finds no way is laid on the board without the autorouter's tracks, and those of them its way comes too near are taken up whole, from pad to pad or fork, and laid again (each connection twice at most, three times as many in all as there were connections), the run ending on the board with the fewest unrouted it reached; tracks laid by hand, and with Route chosen those of the nets not chosen, are never taken up; connections with a pad off the outline are left; the run is one step, a progress box after half a second stops it on Esc or Stop, and at the end a box shows the connections there were, those routed and unrouted, the vias and wire links added and the time taken.
- Wire links: a bare wire, or an insulated flexible one ("insulated" in the file, drawn dashed, any way and up to 150 mm long, over parts and other links), on the side of the parts between two pads with holes (0.8 mm holes, 1.8 mm pads by the project rules, "wireLinks" in the board file), joining them as a track would, for a board with copper on one side where a track cannot go; each has a designator, JP1 or in GOST style XN1 ("designator" in the file), numbered with the wire link parts of the schematic, given by the autorouter (which also names links of older files) and switched with the designator style, printed on the top silkscreen beside the middle of its wire, dragged aside alone like a part's text ("textAt" in the file, its offset) and carried with the link; the pads are through the board in the colour of its copper, the wire is drawn on the top silkscreen; tracks start and end on the pads, the ratsnest counts them as joined, a click on a pad or a frame chooses a link, Delete, Delete all tracks and Hide tracks take it too, and Ctrl+A carries it along; Replace with track in its menu takes the links chosen off and lays their connections by the autorouter with no link, as one step, nothing changing (said in the status bar) when one would be left unrouted.
- Mounting holes: Hole places them, a click each, on the grid; its arrow chooses the diameter from the drill set of the project rules (LUT: 0.6 0.8 1.0 1.2 1.5 2.0 3.0 3.2 4 5 6 8 10 mm; Production: the common ones, 0.6 to 6 mm) or Other… for one typed; the last one chosen stays, 3.2 mm at first. Esc or a short right click ends placing. Holes are chosen, framed and dragged as parts are, and with them; Delete takes them off, with their tracks as a part's. The menu of a hole has Lock or Unlock, its diameter (from the drill set, or typed in place), Copper pad (its diameter typed in place, 0 for none; a pad may go without plating, for a board made at home; a plated hole has its wall drawn as a white line inside the pad, a hole with neither pad nor plating as a grey one; a grey cross marks the middle of every hole), Plated (giving a hole without a pad a 1 mm ring, on both sides; taken off, it takes the ring and its net off too; refused, said in the status bar, when the ring would come onto or too near copper of another net, as are a pad diameter, a hole diameter and a drag that would), Pad side (both, top or bottom, on a board of two sides; a pad of one side is drawn in its copper's colour, and on a board of one side every pad is on that side), Pad net (No net, then the nets of the board, those of the power class first; a pad on a net joins its ratsnest), and Delete. A locked hole is drawn yellow on the screen and is not moved, changed or deleted until unlocked.

The full specification is in [`tz.md`](tz.md) (English) and [`tz.ru.md`](tz.ru.md) (Russian original).

## Building

Needs CMake 3.22+, a C++17 compiler and Qt 6.4+ (Core, Gui, Widgets, LinguistTools, Test). On Ubuntu 24.04:

```
sudo apt install build-essential cmake ninja-build qt6-base-dev qt6-tools-dev qt6-l10n-tools qt6-translations-l10n
./00-Make.sh
```

`00-Make.sh` configures, builds and runs the tests; a build that passed them is copied to `OUT/pocketpcb`. Open `examples/demo/demo.ppcb` to see a small project.

`14-MakeAppImage.sh` then packs that build into `OUT/pocketpcb-<version>-x86_64.AppImage`, one file with Qt, the built-in library and the user guide inside, keeping the last three. It needs `linuxdeploy` and `linuxdeploy-plugin-qt` in `tools/` (kept out of git) and says how to get them when they are missing.
