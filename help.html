PocketPCB

A small editor for schematics and printed circuit boards: draw the schematic, place the parts on the board, lay the tracks, and make the board at a factory or at home by toner transfer.

Projects

  • A project is a folder with its project, schematic and board files and its own copy of the parts it uses. Projects live in the PocketPCB-Projects folder beside the program unless you choose another (Settings > Projects folder).
  • The project is saved with Ctrl+S or the Save button, which stands on red while something is not saved. Backup copies are kept, and the project is autosaved and offered back after a crash.
  • Recent projects and Project properties are the second and third buttons on the strip. A project opened while the board is shown opens on its board.
  • Project properties: the title block, the designator style (R, D, Q, U or GOST R, VD, VT, DD), which sides of the board have copper (both, top only, bottom only), and the net classes (track width, clearance, via).

The window

  • The menus open from the button at the left of the strip, or with F10. Help > User guide (F1) opens this page.
  • The strip of tools: file buttons, Undo and Redo, zoom; in the middle Lock and the tools of the page shown; on the right the panels: Library, Properties, Nets, ERC, BOM.
  • Sheets of the schematic are tabs over the canvas; the board is the last tab.
  • A short right click opens the menu of what is under the mouse.
  • Lock fixes what is chosen so that it cannot be moved or deleted until unlocked; locked parts and holes are yellow on the board.

Schematic

  • Parts come from the Library panel: search, then drag a part onto the sheet, or double click it and click on the sheet.
  • Choose with a click or a frame; drag to move (wires follow, Shift tears them off); R rotates, M mirrors, Del deletes; copy, cut, paste and duplicate work as usual.
  • Wires: W, click by click; Space turns the corner, / slants it. Junction dots appear by themselves.
  • Power symbols: P or the Power button places the last one; its arrow chooses another.
  • Labels: L; labels with the same text are one net. Buses: B. No-connect marks: X.
  • Double click a value, a net name or a label to edit it in place.
  • The Properties panel edits the designator, value, footprint and more of the parts chosen, several at once.
  • The Nets panel lists the nets and gives each a class. The ERC panel lists the problems of the schematic, ringed in red on the sheet. The BOM panel lists the parts and writes TXT, CSV or PDF.
  • File > Print schematic and Export schematic to PDF put the sheets on paper.

Board

From schematic to board

  • Update board from schematic (F8, or its button left of Lock) shows what will change and brings the board in line: new parts are packed to the right of the board, removed ones go, values, designators and nets follow.
  • Light green lines (the ratsnest) show which pads are still to be joined; a line goes out once a track joins its pads.

Seeing the board

  • The Hide silkscreen button, pressed, hides the white and grey drawing of the parts, their designators and values; pads, holes and copper stay, and parts are not chosen then.
  • The Show unrouted connections button, pressed, draws every connection still to be routed bold, with its ends marked.
  • View > Unrouted connections as arcs bends those lines, so that lines from one pad part. The status bar shows how many connections are unrouted, of all of them.

Outline

  • The outline button makes a Rectangular board; its arrow also offers drawing an Outline corner by corner and a Cut-out across the edge.
  • Drag a corner or an edge to change the board; double click a corner to round it; double click an edge to choose the whole outline and move it.

Parts

  • Click, Ctrl+click or a frame to choose; drag to move; R to turn.
  • Designators and values are dragged on their own. The menu of a part shows or hides its value and draws a line from its texts to it.
  • Del moves the parts chosen off the board, to the right of it: parts come and go with the schematic.

Mounting holes

  • The Hole button places holes, a click each; its arrow chooses the diameter from the drill set of the project, or another one.
  • The menu of a hole: diameter, copper pad (its diameter, 0 for none), plated, the side of the pad, its net, lock, delete. A plated hole has a white wall; every hole has a cross in its middle.

Tracks

  • Track (T): click on a pad, a via or a track to start; each click lays a straight piece and one at 45°; a click on copper of the same net ends the track.
  • Space puts the slanted piece first or last; hold Shift for any angle.
  • V puts a via and goes on on the other side; without a track it switches the side. The side you work on is bright, the other darker. The Layer button does the same, and its arrow chooses the side.
  • The width comes from the class of the net. Esc or a right click ends the track.
  • A click on a track chooses it, a double click the whole track; Del removes it. Its menu sets the width (any, typed under Other…) and the side.
  • Rest the mouse on copper for a second to light its whole net and see its name.
